# Standards of Conduct for Volunteers and Organisations Supporting Ukraine

**Draft v0.10 — for consultation**

## Purpose

People and organisations supporting Ukraine are entrusted with money, equipment, information, access and, sometimes, people's safety.

Good intentions are not enough.

Donors should know what their contributions are used for. Recipients should receive what they actually need and be able to rely on it. Volunteers should understand the risks they undertake. Personal and financial interests should be visible where they matter. Legitimate questions should receive proper answers.

This Code establishes a common ethical and professional standard for that work.

It applies to individuals, informal groups, charities, NGOs and other organisations raising or handling funds, supplying goods or services, manufacturing equipment, transporting aid, organising volunteers or otherwise asking the public to contribute money, goods or time in support of Ukraine.

It covers humanitarian, civilian and military support. The nature and purpose of the activity must be described honestly.

---

## Principles

Signatories commit to six principles:

**Purpose** — the needs of those being supported come before personal profile, convenience or publicity.

**Honesty** — fundraising, relationships, interests, capabilities and outcomes are represented truthfully.

**Accountability** — resources entrusted by others remain identifiable, are properly used and are reported.

**Competence** — work is undertaken with appropriate skill, care and regard for its consequences.

**Safety** — equipment and activity are handled so that the people supplying, carrying, using and receiving them are not put at avoidable risk.

**Dignity** — people are treated with respect, their information is protected, and their circumstances are not exploited for fundraising, publicity or personal advantage.

**Openness and correction** — legitimate scrutiny is answered, concerns are raised responsibly and mistakes are corrected.

---

## Status of the Code

This is a voluntary Code of Conduct. Signing it is a public commitment to follow its requirements.

**There is no register, no certifying body and no verification process.** Nobody checks whether a signatory complies. A signatory publishes their own commitment, in their own name, through their own channels, and links to this Code.

That is deliberate. This Code is a standard people hold themselves to and can be held to by others. It is not a badge that confers trust.

Signing is not certification, accreditation, vetting, an audit, or a guarantee that a signatory has never fallen short. Anyone deciding whether to donate, volunteer or partner should judge a signatory by their evidence and conduct, not by the fact that they have signed.

Section 13 governs how signatory status may and may not be described. Section 14 governs versions.

The Code supplements rather than replaces applicable law, regulation, licence conditions, contractual requirements and professional obligations.

Its requirements are proportionate to both scale and risk. A person raising €300 is not expected to maintain the administrative systems of a large NGO, but the underlying obligations of honesty, accountability, competence and care are the same.

The standard of conduct does not change with scale. The records, controls and processes reasonably required to demonstrate it may.

Where this Code requires information, consent, reporting routes or safety information to be provided for the benefit of a recipient or volunteer, it must be communicated in a language and form they can reasonably understand.

This Code applies to support for a country under armed attack, and covers support intended to be used in armed force. It does not treat that purpose as something to be concealed, softened or minimised. It requires it to be described honestly and handled lawfully, and it governs how signatories treat the people they work with, not the conduct of hostilities.

## Definitions

**Aid resources** means money, goods, materials, equipment, services or labour provided for a stated aid or support purpose rather than for the personal benefit of the organiser.

**Appeal** means a discrete request to the public for money, goods, labour or other resources for an identifiable purpose or outcome.

An appeal **closes** on the earliest of: the signatory ceasing to solicit resources for it; the stated target being met; or the stated purpose being achieved or abandoned. Where a signatory has not stated a target or a closing point, an appeal closes when active solicitation for it stops.

**Continuing fund** means an open-ended or recurring fundraiser that receives resources for an ongoing purpose without a defined closing point. This includes permanent donation accounts, jars, subscriptions and standing funds.

**Controlled goods** means goods, software or technology subject to export, trade, sanctions, military or dual-use controls in any applicable jurisdiction.

**Material** means information, conduct or circumstances that could reasonably influence a donor's decision to contribute, a recipient's decision to accept support, a volunteer's decision to participate or travel, or the safety or lawful conduct of the activity.

**Organisation** means a charity, NGO, company, association, partnership, informal group or other body through which two or more people act under a shared identity, management or purpose, whether incorporated or not.

**Public personal support** means money or material benefits solicited from the public wholly or partly to meet a person's own living costs or provide them with personal financial support.

**Recipient** means the person, organisation or unit intended to receive support. **End-user** means the person, organisation or unit intended ultimately to use it.

**Safety-critical** means equipment or activity whose foreseeable failure or misuse could cause death or serious injury.

**Vouch** means an affirmative public representation that another person or organisation is trustworthy, accountable or suitable for others to support.

# 1. Support responds to a real need

### 1.1
Before raising funds or procuring aid, signatories must take reasonable steps to establish that the intended recipient has a current need for what is proposed.

### 1.2
Recipients' needs and operational requirements must take precedence over donor preference, publicity value or an organiser's desire to supply a particular item.

### 1.3
Where circumstances may have changed materially, the need should be reconfirmed before significant procurement or delivery.

Reasonable efforts must be made to avoid unnecessary duplication and waste.

### 1.4
Recipients must be free to decline unsuitable aid, request reasonable changes or report problems without being threatened with loss of future support.

### 1.5
Aid must not be conditional upon a recipient participating in promotional activity, providing personal endorsements, granting unnecessary access, or conferring honours or similar benefits upon an organiser or donor.

This does not prevent reasonable requirements for verification, reporting, audit, acknowledgement of institutional funding or evidence that aid reached its intended purpose.

Such requirements must not unnecessarily compromise the recipient's safety, dignity or operational security.

Signatories must not accept donor conditions that would require otherwise.

### 1.6
Signatories must provide recipients with a reasonable means of reporting defective or unsuitable aid and raising concerns about the conduct of people acting on the signatory's behalf.

The formality of that mechanism may reflect the scale and risk of the operation.

# 2. Fundraising is honest and resources are controlled

### 2.1
Every appeal must state, as far as safety permits:

- what is being funded;
- the amount sought;
- the intended recipient or purpose; and
- any material uncertainty affecting the appeal.

Claims about cost, urgency, need and intended use must be truthful.

### 2.2
Aid resources must remain separately identifiable from personal resources and must not be used for personal expenditure.

This applies whether resources are received through a bank account, payment service, cash, cryptocurrency or another method.

A dedicated payment destination should be used where practicable. Where resources pass through an account or wallet containing other funds, records must allow them to be identified and reconciled.

### 2.3
The published payment destination must correspond to the person or organisation conducting the fundraiser, or the relationship must be disclosed before donations are requested.

Material changes to payment details must be announced through the same channels used for the fundraiser.

### 2.4
Signatories must not send unsolicited private fundraising approaches or payment details to people with whom they have no pre-existing relationship or prior permission for fundraising contact.

This does not prevent an appropriate direct approach to an established donor or other existing contact.

### 2.5
Fundraising identities must not be misleading.

Signatories must not create or operate an account, organisation or campaign that could reasonably be mistaken for another person or organisation.

Another person's or organisation's name, branding or material must not be used for fundraising without appropriate permission and attribution.

### 2.6
Goods purchased with donated resources must be described as donor-funded.

Organisers may take proper credit for sourcing, manufacturing, transporting or delivering aid, but must not imply that they personally financed something paid for by donors.

### 2.7
Where a signatory receives an award, honour, appointment or public recognition connected to donor-funded work, they must not accept or repeat an account of their contribution that materially overstates it.

Awards, honours and press coverage must not be presented as evidence that fundraising has been accounted for.

### 2.8
A material change to the purpose of an appeal or continuing fund must be disclosed before resources are redirected.

Where practicable, donors to a discrete appeal must be given an opportunity to object or request return of their contribution when the new purpose differs materially from the original one.

Surplus resources must be declared and used consistently with the stated purpose unless a change is disclosed on the same basis.

### 2.9
Cash receipts and payments should be recorded contemporaneously.

Cryptocurrency and other digital transfers must be recorded sufficiently to permit reconciliation.

Material payment fees, currency conversions and exchange gains or losses must be reflected in reporting where they affect the resources available for the stated purpose.

### 2.10
A signatory responsible for material aid funds, assets or records must make reasonable contingency arrangements so that they can be identified, safeguarded and accounted for if that person dies or becomes incapable of continuing the work.

# 3. Fundraising is reported and reconciled

### 3.1
Every completed appeal must be followed by a report showing, as applicable:

- how much was raised;
- what was purchased or funded;
- what it cost;
- what happened to any surplus; and
- that the intended recipient or purpose benefited.

### 3.2
Reports must be supported by evidence sufficient for meaningful verification.

Depending on the circumstances, this may include invoices, receipts, payment records, shipping or vehicle documents, handover records, correspondence, photographs or video.

**Photographs, testimonials and delivery videos alone are not financial reconciliation.**

Financial and transactional records supporting reports must be retained for at least six years, or longer where required by applicable law, licence, contract or funding conditions.

### 3.3
Within 60 days of an appeal closing, a signatory must publish either a final report or an interim report.

An interim report must state, as applicable:

- the amount raised;
- expenditure to date;
- what remains outstanding;
- why the appeal cannot yet be fully reconciled; and
- the expected next steps.

A final report must follow when the activity is complete.

### 3.4
Where resources are raised through a continuing fund, the first reporting period begins when the fund first receives resources.

The signatory must publish a financial summary covering each period of no more than three months, stating as applicable:

- opening balance;
- resources received;
- expenditure;
- expenditure broken down into categories sufficiently specific for a reasonable donor to understand how the resources were used; and
- closing balance.

At least once every twelve months, the continuing fund must also be covered by a full report meeting the evidential requirements of clause 3.2.

**Smaller funds.** Where a continuing fund received less than £5,000, or a reasonable equivalent in another currency, during the preceding twelve months, quarterly summaries are not required and a single annual report is sufficient.

Once that threshold is exceeded, quarterly reporting applies from the next reporting period.

### 3.5
Using a continuing fund as the payment destination does not convert a discrete appeal into continuing fundraising.

Where resources are solicited for an identifiable specific purchase, project, recipient or fundraising target, that activity constitutes an appeal for the purposes of clauses 3.1–3.3, regardless of the account, jar, wallet or other payment destination through which the resources are received.

### 3.6
A new appeal must identify any materially outstanding report from an earlier appeal and provide access to the latest interim report.

Where a continuing fund has an overdue quarterly or annual report, that must likewise be identified before a new appeal is launched using that fund, and before any renewed public solicitation for the fund itself.

A live donation feed or list of incoming payments is not a substitute for reporting expenditure.

### 3.7
Sensitive recipient, location or operational information may be withheld.

The reason must be stated where reasonably possible, and non-sensitive financial and procurement information must still be reported.

# 4. Work is competent and aid is fit for purpose

### 4.1
Signatories must not claim qualifications, expertise or experience they do not possess.

Safety-critical work must not be undertaken beyond a person's competence without appropriate supervision or specialist support.

### 4.2
Goods must be safe and suitable for their intended use.

The level of checking required must reflect the foreseeable consequences of failure.

### 4.3
Safety-critical medical, protective and technical equipment must be obtained from sources whose identity and provenance can reasonably be established and checked for authenticity, relevant certification and usable life where applicable.

### 4.4
Manufactured, modified or assembled equipment should be tested appropriately before delivery.

Material limitations in testing must be disclosed to the recipient. Experimental equipment and prototypes must be clearly identified.

### 4.5
Second-hand equipment should be inspected appropriately before supply.

Where safe use requires specialist competence or training, reasonable steps must be taken to establish that the recipient has it or has access to suitable instruction.

### 4.6
If supplied equipment is subsequently found to be materially unsafe, counterfeit or defective, affected recipients must be told promptly.

Where others may possess the same affected product or batch, the risk must be communicated more widely where doing so may prevent harm.

**Reputation must never take precedence over safety.**

# 5. Military, controlled and dual-use support is handled lawfully and transparently

### 5.1
Support must be described according to its actual purpose.

Signatories must not describe military support as humanitarian aid or conceal a military, dual-use or, where relevant, lethal purpose in order to obtain donations, services, transport, payment processing or regulatory treatment that would not otherwise be available.

### 5.2
Before exporting, transferring or arranging the transfer of controlled goods, signatories must take reasonable steps to establish:

- the jurisdictions and controls that apply;
- how the goods or technology are classified;
- whether a licence, authorisation or declaration is required;
- any sanctions or end-use restrictions; and
- the identity and intended use of the end-user.

Where the position is unclear, competent advice or authoritative official guidance must be obtained.

**Where the applicable classification, authorisation or legality of the transfer cannot reasonably be established, the transfer must not proceed until the uncertainty is resolved.**

### 5.3
Military or controlled goods must not knowingly be supplied to an unverified end-user.

Verification must be proportionate to the nature, value and sensitivity of the goods, and sufficient records must be retained to show the basis on which the transfer was made.

### 5.4
Where an intermediary is used, reasonable steps must be taken to establish both the intermediary's role and the intended ultimate end-user.

### 5.5
Documents, descriptions, values, end-use statements and other information provided to authorities, carriers, financial services or other parties must not knowingly be false or misleading.

### 5.6
Where a signatory becomes aware of credible evidence that military or controlled goods have been materially diverted from their stated recipient or purpose, the matter must be documented and investigated.

Further supply must be paused where reasonably necessary to prevent additional diversion, and any reporting obligations arising under applicable law, licence conditions or sanctions must be followed.

A signatory is not automatically responsible for every act of a verified recipient after lawful handover, but credible evidence of diversion must not be ignored.

### 5.7
Signatories must not supply or arrange support that they know, or have reasonable grounds to believe, is intended for use in a manner that would be unlawful, including the deliberate targeting of civilians or other serious violation of the law of armed conflict.

Signatories are not expected to assess operational or targeting decisions, and are not responsible for the lawful conduct of a verified recipient. Credible evidence of unlawful intended use must not be ignored.

# 6. Public personal support is transparent

Being supported financially for Ukraine-related work is not unethical.

Public personal support is treated differently from ordinary salary or unrelated private income because the signatory is asking members of the public to decide whether to provide additional financial support to that person.

This is money donors give to a person rather than to Ukraine. Potential donors are entitled to enough information to make that decision intelligently.

### 6.1
Public personal support must be clearly distinguished from money raised for aid.

### 6.2
A signatory soliciting ongoing public personal support must publish the approximate average amount they receive from that support.

The figure must combine **all public personal-support channels** and should normally be based on the preceding three complete months.

The amount may be rounded to avoid false precision, but the rounding must not materially understate the support received.

For a newly established fundraiser with less than three months' history, the average received to date may be used.

Where support is received in more than one currency, the amounts must be converted on a reasonable and consistently applied basis into the currency in which most of that support is solicited or received.

Receipts must be counted when received or made available to the signatory. A calculation method must not be chosen or applied so as materially to understate the amount disclosed.

**A person who asks the public to fund them personally must tell the public approximately how much the public is already funding them.**

### 6.3
The disclosed amount must be reviewed at least quarterly, and sooner where a significant change would make the published figure materially misleading.

The figure represents support received, not the amount remaining after personal expenditure.

Material recurring benefits in kind must also be disclosed, although they need not be assigned an artificial cash value.

### 6.4
For a one-off public appeal intended wholly or partly for the signatory's personal benefit, the total raised must be published when the appeal closes.

### 6.5
A signatory making an express claim of personal financial hardship or need must disclose any income or support whose omission would make that claim materially misleading.

There is otherwise no general requirement under this Code to publish unrelated private income, savings, household finances or the precise salary received from an employer.

### 6.6
Where publishing the approximate amount would create a specific and credible safety risk, a signatory may withhold the figure.

Where the figure is withheld, the signatory must state publicly that it is withheld on safety grounds, must not imply that no public personal support is received, and must review that decision at least annually and whenever circumstances materially change.

This exception applies to publication of the amount. It does not remove the requirement to keep personal support and aid resources separate, or to account for either.

### 6.7
Personal support and aid resources must be kept and accounted for separately.

Disclosures must appear where potential personal donors are reasonably likely to see them, rather than merely somewhere they are technically available.

# 7. Conflicts of interest and financial relationships are disclosed

### 7.1
Paid roles, commissions, benefits in kind and other material financial relationships relevant to apparently independent or voluntary work must be disclosed, together with who provides them.

### 7.2
Material conflicts of interest must be disclosed and managed.

This includes a financial or significant personal interest in a supplier, contractor, recipient, partner or other decision from which the signatory or someone closely connected to them may benefit.

### 7.3
Signatories responsible for selecting suppliers, partners or recipients must act in the interests of the stated purpose rather than their own financial or personal interests.

### 7.4
Bribes, kickbacks and undisclosed commissions must not be offered, sought or accepted.

### 7.5
An audience must not be led to believe that an endorsement, recommendation or opinion is independent when a material undisclosed relationship exists.

**Being paid is not the problem. Concealment is.**

# 8. People, information and dignity are protected

### 8.1
People must be treated with dignity and must not be exploited for fundraising or publicity.

Photographs, personal stories, names and medical information must be used only with appropriate informed consent and in a manner that is accurate and respectful.

Consent for one purpose must not be assumed to cover unrelated future use.

### 8.2
Signatories must not exploit a person's dependency, vulnerability, status or access to aid for sexual, financial, personal or reputational advantage.

A signatory with staff, volunteers or others acting on its behalf must take proportionate steps to prevent and address abuse, harassment, sexual exploitation and discriminatory conduct.

### 8.3
Personal information must be collected only where reasonably necessary, protected appropriately and retained only for as long as reasonably necessary or legally required.

Donor identities and unredacted payment information are private by default.

Donors may be thanked publicly where they have consented and doing so creates no unreasonable risk.

### 8.4
Published information must not unnecessarily compromise operational security, including current positions, movements, unit strength or delivery routes and timings.

### 8.5
People invited or encouraged to travel to Ukraine must receive an honest account of the work, foreseeable risks and available support before committing.

Where a signatory organises or facilitates travel into a high-risk area or near active hostilities, the material risk information must be provided in writing, in a language and form the traveller can reasonably understand, and the traveller's acknowledgement retained.

Where relevant, this must cover:

- the work to be undertaken;
- likely proximity to hostilities;
- insurance;
- emergency and evacuation arrangements;
- significant legal requirements; and
- what assistance realistically exists in the event of serious injury or death.

Where no meaningful provision exists, that must be said plainly.

### 8.6
Untrained or inexperienced civilians must not be exposed to unnecessary frontline risk for publicity, content, status or a donor experience.

Anyone arranging travel must maintain appropriate emergency and next-of-kin information.

### 8.7
People must not be pressured into dangerous work because they have volunteered, donated or offered to help.

Volunteers must not be shamed for resting, withdrawing or returning home.

### 8.8
A signatory appointing people to positions involving significant trust, vulnerable people or substantial unsupervised responsibility should undertake proportionate reference and safeguarding checks.

A signatory must not knowingly conceal **substantiated serious safeguarding concerns** when providing a reference or other information about a person's suitability for comparable work, where disclosure is lawful, fair and reasonably necessary to protect others.

Unsupported allegations or unresolved rumours must not be presented as established fact.

### 8.9
Credible safeguarding concerns must be acted upon and, where required or reasonably necessary to protect others, referred to an appropriate authority or organisation.

---

# 9. Endorsement and partnership carry responsibility

### 9.1
Before promoting another appeal, signatories must take reasonable care not to promote something they know, or have reasonable grounds to suspect, is materially misleading, unsafe or seriously unaccountable.

### 9.2
Joint appeals, formal partnerships and continuing fundraising relationships require proportionate due diligence.

This must include considering whether the other party can account for previous fundraising and whether material unresolved concerns exist.

### 9.3
A public vouch must have a reasonable evidential basis.

Friendship, popularity, awards, press coverage or having met somebody are not substitutes for evidence of accountable practice.

The fact that another person or organisation has signed this Code is not by itself evidence of accountable practice.

### 9.4
A material endorsement or vouch later shown to be materially wrong must be corrected or withdrawn publicly and promptly.

Deleting the original statement without correcting the impression it created is not sufficient.

### 9.5
Signatories must not imply that accountability established for one person, organisation or fund extends to separately controlled fundraising or activity for which that accountability has not been established.

---

# 10. Questions, complaints and concerns are handled responsibly

### 10.1
Signatories should provide an accessible and monitored route through which donors, recipients, volunteers and others can raise questions or concerns.

The route should be communicated to recipients and volunteers where reasonably relevant, in a language and form they can reasonably understand.

A signatory with staff, volunteers or others acting on its behalf must provide a proportionate means for them to report serious misconduct confidentially and without retaliation.

### 10.2
Reasonable questions about fundraising, expenditure, procurement and delivery must be answered openly and within a reasonable time.

A material accountability question should normally be acknowledged within seven days.

Where a substantive answer cannot then be provided, the acknowledgement should explain when an answer is expected or why a timescale cannot yet reasonably be given.

Where a question is public and can safely be answered publicly, the substantive answer should ordinarily be public.

### 10.3
Signatories must not block, report, exclude, intimidate or otherwise retaliate against somebody solely for asking a legitimate accountability question or raising a concern in good faith.

Persistence, disagreement and uncomfortable questions are not by themselves harassment or abuse.

Supporters must not be encouraged to target people raising legitimate concerns.

### Raising concerns about others

Clauses 10.4–10.8 apply to signatories when they raise concerns about others.

They are also offered to the wider community as a description of responsible conduct.

### 10.4
Where there is no immediate material risk and the apparent problem could reasonably be an error, omission or gap in practice, a direct approach should normally precede public accusation.

### 10.5
Immediate public warning may be appropriate where delay creates a material risk, including where:

- donations are actively being sought on materially misleading information;
- money or property is at immediate risk;
- unsafe equipment may be supplied or used;
- people may be exposed to avoidable danger;
- evidence may disappear; or
- reasonable attempts to obtain an answer have already been ignored.

### 10.6
Concerns must distinguish established fact, reasonable inference and allegation.

Where possible, they must identify specific, checkable conduct rather than rely upon character, personality, politics or reputation.

Serious allegations about a person's conduct, motives or affiliations must have a reasonable evidential basis.

### 10.7
A material allegation found to be wrong must be corrected or withdrawn promptly and with reasonable prominence.

### 10.8
A signatory is not required by this Code to conduct a prolonged public campaign or to engage with threats, harassment or abuse.

Providing evidence to an affected recipient, organisation, regulator, law-enforcement body or other appropriate authority and then disengaging can be responsible conduct.

The conduct of the person raising a concern and the conduct of the person answering it must be judged independently.

---

# 11. Fraud and serious misconduct are addressed

### 11.1
Credible evidence of fraud, theft, deliberate diversion, corruption, serious safeguarding misconduct or other material misuse must be acted upon.

Appropriate action may include protecting remaining assets, preserving evidence, informing affected parties and reporting the matter to an appropriate authority.

### 11.2
Material wrongdoing that a signatory knows, or has reasonable grounds to conclude, has occurred must not be concealed solely to protect a reputation.

Public disclosure may be delayed or limited where necessary to avoid materially prejudicing an investigation, recovery action, legal process, operational security or somebody's safety.

---

# 12. Mistakes are acknowledged and corrected

### 12.1
An honest mistake, administrative failure or gap in practice is not by itself evidence of dishonesty.

### 12.2
When a material shortcoming is identified, the expected response is:

**Acknowledge it. Explain what will be done. Correct it.**

### 12.3
Where others may have been materially affected, reasonable steps should be taken to communicate the correction to them.

### 12.4
Repeated failure to correct the same problem, deliberate concealment or retaliation against legitimate scrutiny is more serious than an isolated error acknowledged and corrected in good faith.

---

# 13. Signing, and describing signatory status

### 13.1
A person or organisation signs this Code by publishing a statement of commitment through the channels they use for their Ukraine-support work, identifying the version accepted and linking to or reproducing the Code text.

There is no application, no approval and no central list.

### 13.2
A signatory may describe themselves as:

> **Signatory to the Standards of Conduct for Volunteers and Organisations Supporting Ukraine, Version [X].**

A shorter form is acceptable provided it identifies the Code and the version accepted.

### 13.3
Signatories must not describe or present themselves, by reason of signing, as:

- certified;
- accredited;
- approved;
- verified;
- audited;
- vetted;
- registered;
- guaranteed trustworthy; or
- any equivalent description.

### 13.4
Signatories must not create or use a badge, logo, mark, graphic or wording that could reasonably imply that any body has verified, checked, approved or endorsed them.

### 13.5
Where a signatory is asked whether they have signed, they should be able to point to their own published commitment.

### 13.6
A person or organisation that no longer accepts these commitments must promptly remove or amend their published statement and cease presenting themselves as a signatory.

### 13.7
Misrepresenting signatory status, or continuing to present as a signatory after ceasing to accept the Code, is itself a material breach of it.

# 14. Versions and amendments

### 14.1
Every published version of this Code must carry:

- a version number;
- a publication date;
- an effective date; and
- a public record of what changed.

### 14.2
Changes are classified as:

**Editorial** — corrections, clarification or restructuring that do not materially alter signatory commitments.

**Material** — changes introducing, removing or materially altering substantive commitments.

### 14.3
Editorial changes do not require anything of existing signatories.

### 14.4
Following a material revision, a signatory should review the new version and update their published statement within 60 days of its effective date.

A signatory who does not accept the revised version should either state which version they accept or cease presenting themselves as a signatory.

### 14.5
A published statement of commitment must identify the version accepted, so that anyone reading it can see what was actually undertaken.

# Using this Code

Nobody checks whether signatories comply. That job belongs to the people they ask for money, goods, time and trust.

This Code is written so it can be used as a set of questions. Anyone may use it, whether or not they have signed anything.

**If you are thinking of donating:**

- Ask to see the report for the last appeal. Photographs and delivery videos are not financial reconciliation.
- If it is a permanent jar, subscription or standing fund, ask where the quarterly summaries and the annual report are.
- Check that the payment destination matches the person or organisation asking.
- If the appeal is for military or dual-use support, check that it is described as such.

**If someone asks the public to fund them personally:**

- Ask roughly how much they already receive from the public.
- Ask whether personal support and aid money are kept separately.
- If they claim hardship, that claim should account for what they already receive.

**If you are being asked to travel or volunteer:**

- Ask for the risks in writing, including insurance, evacuation and what happens if you are seriously injured or killed.
- If the honest answer is that no provision exists, you are entitled to hear that before you commit.

**If someone tells you they have signed this Code:**

- Ask them to show you the commitment they published, and which version it names.
- Signing is a self-declaration. Nobody verified it. It is a starting point for questions, not an answer to them.

**A signatory who resents these questions is telling you something.**

# Commitment

By signing this Code, we undertake to:

- put the purpose of the work and the needs of recipients before personal interest or publicity;
- describe honestly what we do, how we are funded and the interests that may affect us;
- account for resources entrusted to us;
- work within our competence and take reasonable care over what we supply;
- handle military and controlled support lawfully and transparently;
- protect the safety, privacy and dignity of the people with whom we work;
- answer legitimate questions and raise concerns responsibly; and
- acknowledge and correct material shortcomings.

We do not claim perfection.

We accept accountability.

**Trust in Ukraine-support work should rest on evidence and conduct, not reputation alone.**
