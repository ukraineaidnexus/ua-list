# What I saw at Lviv Volunteer Kitchen

### Douglas Hall 22 September 2026

This is a response to a claim made on X on 21 September 2026 that Lviv Volunteer
Kitchen (LVK) was "a minor operation run by two ego driven women who liked to
yell at volunteers", with no international footprint and a church group as its
funding basis. I've been asked what I think of that, having volunteered there.

This is what I saw. It's a first-hand account, not an argument. Where something
is second-hand or I'm unsure, I'll make that clear.

I volunteered at LVK from 28 November 2022 to New Year's Eve 2022, with a break
away from Lviv between 14 and 25 December, as well as two days away in early
December as I took a bus back to Poland to transport a car to Khmelnytskyi for
another volunteer group. I went back for a couple of days in late May 2023. I
was also at the NAFO gathering in Vilnius in July 2023. That's the extent of
what I can speak to directly.

## Where I stand and why I'm saying this now

I should be upfront about my own history here. I supported Front Line Kitchen. For a
long time I had a pinned post on X listing fundraisers I trusted, and Richard's
was one of them. I removed his entry after the split with LVK in September
2023.

I set up my website, Ukraine Aid Nexus, earlier this year, partly because of
the questions others have repeatedly raised about Richard Woodruff and the
questions around his financing, personal expenses, lack of transparency and so
forth. I wanted a list of organisations and volunteers, primarily highlighting
Ukrainian ones, particularly those that don't have much visibility on social
media, doing my best to ascertain that could be trusted and that showed their
receipts. The draftcode of conduct for volunteers I've been working on since
June was also shaped, more than anything else, by Richard's perceived conduct.

I haven't said anything publicly until now because almost everything I could
say would have been speculation. The one thing that wasn't was Christmas 2022.
Richard went back to the UK, told me not to tell anyone he wasn't at the
kitchen, and had me send him photos and content from the kitchen. His account
then posted that content as though he were still there. At the time I didn't
think much of it. It was only in a conversation last weekend that someone
pointed out what that showed about how he operated, even then, and urged me to
put it on the record. So I did, and this statement follows from that.

## How I got there

I was already planning a trip to Ukraine in November 2022 to visit people I
knew. I'd been given a pointer by someone (I forget who) about volunteering
opportunities at the Front Line Kitchen in Lviv, so I messaged RIchard to
enquire further. He said no need to plan anything, just turn up
and help, and gave me the address.

When I arrived in Lviv I checked into my accommodation and walked over to the
address I was given. My first impression wasn't great. One of the women running
it asked why I was there. I'd assumed Richard had told them I was coming. It
was confusing for a few minutes, then they put me to work prepping vegetables.

## The founders

They spoke almost no English and I spoke no Ukrainian, so there was obvious
friction when they tried to explain what needed doing. If you were doing
something wrong, they'd certainly tell you, usually with much exclamation and
plenty of gestures about how to wield the knife or cut things up. But I never
once saw anyone shout at anyone. The "liked to yell at volunteers" description
doesn't match anything I witnessed. They were certainly not rude or unkind to
me. I think they were always a bit bemused that these foreigners kept turning up to 
help (more so from a why on earth would you come here to help?).
I'm afraid I don't have much else to add here, anything else would be speculation.

## The volunteers

Volunteers came
from several countries. I specifically remember a Frenchman and an American on
that first day, alongside local Ukrainians. As far as I know, neither the
Frenchman nor the American was there because of Richard. I know for certain the
American wasn't, as he explained his backstory in Ukraine to me over the
following weeks.

The work was hard and cold, but the locals were warm and welcoming. They'd
repeatedly make tea for the volunteers and make snacks for us, occasionally we
even got to try some of their homemade borshch. 

Some foreign volunteers who'd found the place through Twitter would turn up for
half a day and never come back. In fairness, I think some of them expected a
kitchen where you cooked meals, and it was really a vegetable prep operation,
which was never made very clear. My impression was that a few others came
mainly to get photos of themselves helping the war effort and then move on. I
imagine the founders found both frustrating. That said, it wasn't the majority.
Most people who turned up worked hard. I enjoyed it and met people from all
over.

The volunteers weren't just foreigners either. Locals came in after their day
jobs and worked into the evening. I remember an older woman who spoke very good
English, a piano teacher I think, and a local lawyer who came in after work.
I'm sure there were others I'm forgetting.

There was an occasion when a UK volunteer (I believe a UK veteran based on my
recollection of the conversation), who was playing Ukrainian music on a
bluetooth speaker that apparently had expletives in, which the ladies of the
kitchen were not happy with and asked him to change the music to something
else.

## "A minor operation"

I was told, by locals and by Richard himself, that the kitchen had been running
since Russia's invasion began in 2014. That's second-hand, but it came from more
than one source, including Richard.

What they actually did, as I saw it. They made dried meals for the front, the
kind you add hot water to. Borshch and several other dishes. The basement
kitchen was where vegetables were prepped, cut and sliced. They had two dryers
on site, each roughly fridge-sized. Prepped vegetables also went into cars and
were driven off to another site with one or two much larger dryers. I never
visited that site, but I saw photos of it, I saw the vegetables being loaded
into cars, and I saw bags of dried product coming back.

The kitchen itself was in a basement. Small, but you could fit about 8 people
working in it, with one or two more working outside despite the cold. 

There was also a second building on site, essentially a converted garage (8 x
12 metres or so) as a storeroom, packed with dried goods, and with people
working in it. My recollection is that this is where the finished bags were
assembled. There was a further storage space, I think above and to the left of
the kitchen basement, holding dried herbs for tea. I'm less sure of exactly
where that was, or its actual purpose, but that's my recollection.

On top of that, from photos and reports at the time, locals were baking cakes
and energy bars at home and bringing them in. I didn't see the baking myself,
but I did see items being regularly delivered by car and foot. Some of the home
made cakes and the like came with pictures and messages drawn by local children
for the soldiers.

So the kitchen was one part of a larger operation across several sites and
people's homes.

Whatever else you want to say about LVK, "a minor operation run by two ego
driven women who liked to yell at volunteers" isn't an accurate description of
what I saw in December 2022.

## Funding

It's true they held cake sales at the local church on Sundays to raise money for
the kitchen. It wasn't the only source. They had a Facebook page where they
posted what they needed, and every day I was there, locals were turning up on
foot or in cars dropping off supplies. Military volunteers and others were
loading up vehicles with finished product and driving it away. So "their funding
basis was a church group" is at best a partial description.

It's also true that Richard raised a lot of money for them. My recollection is
that this really got going around the time I was there, late 2022. The kitchen
was starting to get visibility in the NAFO community on Twitter, and it looked
like a good cause to support. If they needed something, for instance an
industrial vegetable slicer, Richard would put a call out on Twitter, people
would send money, and it seemed to happen quickly. I have a recollection that he
gave the founders access to a UK card of his to spend on what they needed, it
may have been a barclays debit card, but also I have recollections of
conversations about revolut, so I'm not certain. I assume his fundraising was a
significant addition to their income. I had no visibility into any of it and I'm
not claiming otherwise.

Separately, another UK volunteer who was there regularly gave the kitchen
several hundred pounds in cash as a personal donation after Richard had gone
back to the UK for Christmas. Beyond that I had no involvement in money or saw
any financial transactions.

## Richard's role

To be clear: Richard was not a founder of the kitchen. It was founded and run by
two Ukrainian women and had existed for years before he arrived. His description
of his involvement at the kitchen varied considerably. In August 2023 at the
time of the split from the kitchen, he himself told his "Volunteering Ukraine"
chatgroup that h'd simply just been their social media manager for a while.

Media reports on the BBC, Daily Telegraph and others suggest that the
involvement was greater than that. For example
https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-sussex-64757909 "Chef helps feed Ukrainian
soldiers fighting russia" ... "As well as cooking for troops, Mr Woodruff,
founder of Front Line Kitchen, organises the arrival of medicine, drones,
vehicles", and
https://www.ukrinform.net/rubric-society/3805342-richard-woodruff-british-volunteer.html
"By the end of my time there, I was managing the whole process every day. I was
in control of organizing the premises. I did 100% of the fundraising. I was
funding the whole operation, bringing all the volunteers, and doing day-to-day
running"

Day to day, in December 2022, he spent a lot of his time in the kitchen sitting
on the sofa with the founders. They were clearly friendly and got on well. He
didn't talk to me much about what he was doing with them. He was in the kitchen
as a presence more than as a worker. He did some prep, but a lot less than the
rest of us. I don't say that to be rude, it's just what I saw.

He'd typically arrive around lunchtime or later. Most of us finished around 5 or
6pm, and most evenings a group of us, Richard included, would go out for dinner
and drinks. He often said he couldn't believe how cheap it was to eat out, what
an amazing country it was, that sort of thing.

## May 2023

I was back at the kitchen for a couple of days in May, on my way
home from delivering vehicles to Kyiv. The operation had grown since December.
There were more volunteers, working outside as well as in the basement. The
courtyard had changed: there was a temporary structure I took to be extra
storage, and a covered open-sided area with more tables where people were
prepping outside. While I was there I heard the site was closing and
the kitchen was moving elsewhere. It's now at another location. I've not been
there and can't speak to it, but as I understand it the operation continues.

On that visit I dion't recall Richard working at the kitchen, althiough I think he
must have come by briefly at some point, as a group of us went out again, him
included, for dinner and drinks.

## Vilnius and the split

In July 2023 (8th - 10th)  NAFO held a gathering in Vilnius. I was there
myself, and I saw Richard, one of the kitchen's founders, and Ernest together
at the event. On 15 August 2023 the @LVKitchen2024 account was created, and on
the same day Richard's own account in the volunteer WhatsApp group was that
Lyuda, omne fo the founders, had returned his bank card. Around two months
later, on 12 September 2023, the founders published their statement distancing
LVK from Richard, and he responded the next day. I'm not going to guess at what
happened in between. I wasn't part of it and I don't know.

## The volunteer WhatsApp groups

In 2023 there were two WhatsApp groups for volunteers around Richard's network.
I was a member of both. I left them after the split. Volunteers were having a go
at each other, there was talk of suing the kitchen, and when I said there should
be more accountability I was asked whether I no longer trusted Richard. I still
have exports of those two group chats.

I later deleted my WhatsApp account entirely, which means I no longer have my
direct messages with Richard. A lot was discussed in those. I don't remember the
specifics well enough to say anything about them, so I won't.

## Since I started posting about this on X

Richard said a couple of weeks or ago that he was leaving Twitter. He has since
come back several times to comment on posts about him. My own public comments
began with a thead about OPSec and some replies about his funding more
generally. Since then he has tried to phone me and has messaged me privately. I
haven't read or answered those messages. Anything he has to say about what I've
written, he can say in public, where people can see it.


## What I'm not claiming

I'm not claiming to know why LVK and Richard split, what happened to the money
he raised, or what's happened at the kitchen since May 2023. I'm describing
what I saw on the days I was there.

This document may be updated if further recollections come to mind or if
